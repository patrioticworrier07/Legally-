The supreme law

Constitution of India

The Constitution is much broader than the Fundamental Rights chapter. Use official text for the complete, amended wording of every Article.

PART III

Fundamental Rights · Articles 12–35

Equality (14–18), freedoms and safeguards (19–22), protection against exploitation (23–24), freedom of religion (25–28), cultural and educational rights (29–30), and constitutional remedies (32). Article 21A addresses education for children aged 6–14.

Read official Constitution ↗
PART IVA

Fundamental Duties · Article 51A

Eleven duties include respecting the Constitution and national symbols, protecting sovereignty, promoting harmony, preserving heritage, protecting the environment, developing scientific temper and safeguarding public property.

Read official Constitution ↗
PART IV

Directive Principles · Articles 36–51

Guiding principles for governance, including social welfare, equal justice, public health and environmental protection. They are not enforceable in the same way as Fundamental Rights.

Read official Constitution ↗
PART V–VI

Union and States

Constitutional provisions cover the President, Parliament, courts, Governors, State legislatures and High Courts, among other institutions.

Read official Constitution ↗
PART XVIII

Emergency provisions · Articles 352–360

Constitutional rules concerning national emergency, President's Rule in States, and financial emergency. Their operation depends on the precise text and legal context.

Read official Constitution ↗
PART XX

Constitutional amendment · Article 368

Sets out the constitutional amendment process, including requirements that vary with the subject of the amendment.

Read official Constitution ↗
This is a topic map, not a reproduction of every Article. The full Constitution contains many Parts, Articles and Schedules, and has been amended over time. Consult the latest official consolidated text for exact wording and current status.
Criminal law framework

New laws and legacy laws

India's principal criminal-law framework changed on 1 July 2024. Which statute applies can depend on the date of the alleged conduct and applicable savings/transitional provisions.

01
Substantive criminal law

Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS)

Defines offences and punishments. It replaced the IPC for matters governed by the new law, subject to applicable transition rules.

Official India Code record ↗
02
Procedure

Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS)

Governs criminal procedure, including investigation, arrest, remand, bail and trial. It replaced CrPC for matters governed by the new framework.

Find official text on India Code ↗
03
Evidence

Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 (BSA)

Governs rules of evidence, including provisions relevant to electronic and digital records. It replaced the Indian Evidence Act, subject to transition rules.

Find official text on India Code ↗
↺
Legacy statute

Indian Penal Code, 1860 (IPC)

Important for historical case references and some proceedings governed by applicable savings or transition provisions. Do not assume every current case uses IPC sections.

Ministry of Home Affairs law resources ↗
↺
Legacy procedure

Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 (CrPC)

Former criminal procedure code. Older cases and legal materials may still refer to CrPC; identify the applicable procedural law before relying on a section number.

Ministry of Home Affairs law resources ↗

Cyber legislation

Information Technology Act, 2000

India's core information-technology statute, including provisions relating to electronic records and specified cyber offences. Applicability depends on facts and amendments.

Know the distinction

Bailable vs non-bailable offences

“Non-bailable” does not mean bail is impossible. “Bailable” does not remove every procedural requirement.

How classification works

Under the applicable procedural law and its schedules, offences are classified as bailable or non-bailable. Classification is specific to the offence and statutory version.

Bailable offence

Bail is generally a right when the accused is prepared to furnish the required bail, subject to statutory conditions and procedure.

Check: the exact offence section, relevant schedule, any special statute and current procedural law.

This guide cannot classify a real case without the exact section, facts, applicable law and jurisdiction. Special statutes can impose additional bail restrictions.

Digital rights & responsibility

Information Technology Act, 2000

Cyber incidents may also involve the BNS, data-protection rules or other special laws. The exact facts matter.

Section 43

Unauthorised acts involving computer resources

Addresses specified unauthorised acts involving computers or networks and provides for compensation in the statutory circumstances.

Section 66

Computer-related offences

Criminal consequences may apply when conduct described in section 43 is done dishonestly or fraudulently, as the statute specifies.

Sections 66C / 66D

Identity theft & personation fraud

Specific provisions address fraudulent use of electronic signatures/passwords/unique identification features and cheating by personation using a computer resource.

Section 67

Obscene material in electronic form

Regulates publishing or transmitting specified obscene material in electronic form, subject to statutory definitions and exceptions.

Section 69A

Blocking directions

Provides a statutory framework for blocking public access to information by the government, subject to the Act and rules.

Section 79

Intermediary liability framework

Sets conditional safe-harbour rules for intermediaries, subject to statutory requirements and applicable rules.

Section summaries are simplified for orientation and are not a substitute for reading the amended Act, rules and relevant judgments.
